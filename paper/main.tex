\title{Evidence-Bounded Visible Wound Geometry and Conservative Deposition Planning: A Computational Evaluation}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Diana Rold\'an}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Tecnol\'ogico de Monterrey\\Monterrey, Mexico}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This study asks whether available wound observations support direct
reference-surface filling or instead require conservative staged planning with
an externally specified final target. We integrate sealed evaluations of
visible-surface shape, synthetic sensor routing, calibration failure
detection, healthy-surface extrapolation, and an offline planning adapter.
The evaluation uses synthetic Syn3DWound data, selected author-published SALVE
silicone-phantom reconstructions, and procedural software fixtures. SALVE
shape comparisons use ground-truth similarity alignment and therefore do not
demonstrate independent metric-scale recovery. The clean-measurement gate
accepted 0/24 variants; on the sealed healthy-disc TEST split the frozen
thin-plate-spline method had 0.042\% macro MAE of hole diameter, while
uncertainty coverage was 88.32\%, below its 90\% target. The frozen
18-case carved-wound comparison failed the predefined direct-reference gate.
The resulting computational architecture is staged partial filling with an
operator-defined final target. The safety layer behaved as designed: the
observable router rejected every misaligned, noisy, and low-coverage depth
route context (4/4 each), the strict holdout detected 41/42 configurations
with at least 5\% incremental volume error at 2/48 clean false alarms, and
all five offline integration cases reached their expected decision with no
boundary violations. No clinical validation, physical printing,
real D405 wound acquisition, or CoppeliaSim robot execution is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research Question and Scope}

The central question is whether the available observations justify filling
toward a surface inferred from surrounding healthy skin, or whether the
system should use conservative staged partial filling and require an
operator-defined final target. This is a computational evidence synthesis,
not a clinical trial or a report of autonomous printing.

The project is an uncertainty-aware multimodal computational framework for
visible 3D wound reconstruction, sensor routing, failure detection, and
conservative surface-conformal deposition planning. Historical learned
models and planner components remain in the repository, but are not evidence
for the results below. The evaluation does not claim CT-style reconstruction,
exactly eight orthogonal physical measurements, clinical wound-volume
validity, or a recovered pre-injury anatomy.

The contribution is therefore an evidence-bounded decision framework rather
than a claim of accurate autonomous filling. Its value lies in identifying
when the observations are insufficient and refusing, in a traceable way, to
proceed: (i) an observable sensor router that rejects geometrically
unsupported or miscalibrated routes without ground-truth access; (ii) a
calibration-safety evaluation showing that consequence-relevant faults are
detected far more reliably than total-error faults; (iii) frozen negative
results, including the clean-measurement and direct-reference gates, that
show the pre-wound surface is not identifiable from current observations; and
(iv) a planning adapter that converts these findings into staged, bounded,
operator-targeted simulation plans.

\section{Data and Evaluation}

Syn3DWound provides rendered RGB, shaded labels, cropped meshes, and camera
parameters. Depth maps used in sensor-route tests are rendered from source
meshes and are expressed in dataset units; no native physical depth or
certified scale is available. The selected RGB shape comparison is evaluated
after a similarity alignment and normalized by wound width.

SALVE provides silicone-phantom RGB, camera information, scanner geometry, and
author-published reconstructions. The reported comparisons use GT-derived
similarity alignment and are classified as
\texttt{GT\_SIMILARITY\_ALIGNED\_SHAPE\_EVALUATION}. Independent metric-scale
recovery remains not proven. The requested Seymour II acquisition was not
available from the dataset authors.

Sensor routing and calibration-safety evidence use synthetic configurations.
The strict holdout comprises 48 clean configurations, 168 fault
configurations, 24 design-stress configurations, and 480 diagnostic wound
subsets. These are not independent subjects. Healthy-disc extrapolation uses
a frozen global thin-plate-spline method and a sealed TEST split of 55 patches
from one held-out body model. The final architecture comparison contains 18
synthetically carved cases across two anatomical regions and three edge
deformation conditions. A separate deterministic software demonstration
contains five integration cases.

Metrics are reported in their declared units: fractions of wound width,
fractions of hole diameter, configuration ratios, dataset units, or
GT-aligned SALVE millimeters. Repeated views, pixels, subsets, patches, and
patch-ring records are correlated. Population-level confidence intervals are
therefore not reported. Numerators, denominators, aggregation, data origin,
and confidence-interval decisions are listed in
\texttt{tables/metric\_audit.csv}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Visible Shape and Sensor Routing}

The selected Syn3DWound shape comparison reports mean and P95 point-to-surface
errors of 3.90\% and 5.85\% of wound width, respectively, and 92.64\%
completeness at a 5\%-width tolerance. These are shape metrics after
evaluation-only similarity alignment, not independent metric-scale
measurements. The focused route comparison contains 252 correlated
route/subset results across four sequence contexts. RGB-only geometry is
rejected for insufficient support; mesh-rendered depth routes do not establish
physical sensor performance (Figs.~\ref{fig:architecture}--\ref{fig:routes}).

On the selected 50-frame SALVE PIS4 Accuracy split, the iPhone/Neuralangelo
comparison has a 0.0956\,mm mean point-to-surface distance and 99.57\%
completeness at 1\,mm. The Logitech/NeuS-facto comparison has 0.1372\,mm and
99.15\%, respectively. Both values are shape-only after GT similarity
alignment. Neither supports automatic independent metric-scale recovery.

\subsection{Calibration and Reference-Surface Validity}

The strict calibration holdout detected 73/112, 53/86, and 24/29 total-error
configurations at 5\%, 10\%, and 20\% consequence thresholds. Matched-clean
incremental recall was 41/42, 28/29, and 16/16. Clean false alarms occurred
in 2/48 configurations. Despite these descriptive results, the engineering
gate failed; configuration counts are not independent trials
(Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}).

The clean-measurement validity gate accepted 0 of 24 evaluated variants.
Unobserved healthy-skin closure and unstable extrapolation dominate the
uncertainty. The resulting quantities are computed reference volumes, not
clinical wound volumes. In the separate healthy-disc test, the frozen global
TPS method achieved macro MAE of 0.0420\% and macro P95 of 0.0940\% of hole
diameter, while pointwise uncertainty coverage was 88.32\% against a 90\%
target. The test contains one held-out body model and is only a lower-bound
estimate of difficulty; it does not recover pre-wound anatomy
(Figs.~\ref{fig:ambiguity} and \ref{fig:hidden}).

\subsection{Architecture Decision and Offline Demonstration}

The frozen 18-case direct-reference comparison failed the declared decision
gate, including failures under moderate edge deformation. The selected
architecture is therefore
\texttt{STAGED\_FILL\_WITH\_OPERATOR\_FINAL\_TARGET}. This is a product-policy
decision for further validation, not evidence that staged filling is
clinically safe. The five-case offline integration demonstration completed
its expected software decisions and recorded a three-stage plan/update trace;
it did not execute CoppeliaSim, robot motion, or material deposition
(Fig.~\ref{fig:staged}).

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

The available data do not identify a true pre-wound healthy surface or
certified physical scale. Synthetic depth is mesh-rendered, and SALVE
millimeter metrics follow GT similarity alignment. Small and correlated
evaluation groups preclude broad population inference. No real D405 wound
data, physical sensor evaluation, CoppeliaSim robot execution, clinical
validation, or physical printing was performed. Printing remains disabled.

Under the predefined decision rule, the evidence does not support direct
reference-surface-only filling. The selected computational architecture is
staged partial filling with an operator-defined final target. A source-verified
literature review, venue formatting, and final author/editorial review remain
before manuscript submission; no additional synthetic experiment is proposed.
